# ARI3218: Rubric for Reflective Diary Entry 1

*This rubric covers Reflective Diary Entry 1. It may be updated for the other diary entries and for the final project.*

## What is assessed

Reflective Diary Entry 1 covers Sessions 01 to 04 and is due on Monday 23 November 2026. It carries 5 % of the unit mark and is marked out of 100. The specifications for the entry are on the VLE.

The three parts of the entry (the written entry, the slides and the video) are marked together against the three criteria below. The slides and the video should make the reasoning and the evidence clearer, not repeat the text.

## Criteria and weights

| Criterion | Weight | What it covers in Entry 1 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reasoning | 35 % | The assessment of the capability pattern against an alternative, the map on the four EA layers, and the EU AI Act classification |
| Evidence | 35 % | The reproduced baseline, the data check, the reproducible container, and the monitoring numbers |
| Insight | 30 % | The reflection: what surprised you or went wrong, what you learnt, and what evidence would change your mind |

Each criterion is marked within one of the four bands below. The entry's mark is the weighted sum of the three criterion marks.

## Level descriptors

### Reasoning (35 %)

- *Insufficient (below 40 %)*: choices are stated without alternatives; the capability-pattern assessment or the AI Act classification is missing, or asserted without support.
- *Threshold (40 to 54 %)*: the capability pattern in the task card and one alternative are named, and a conclusion is given; the EA map places the main components; the risk tier is named with the article or annex it relies on.
- *Good (55 to 69 %)*: the two patterns are compared on all four points (latency budget, running cost per day, who owns the decision, what happens when it fails), using the measured numbers from task 4; the EA map shows where the package, the container, the database and the dashboard sit, and who acts on the output; the provider and the deployer are identified correctly.
- *Excellent (70 % and above)*: the trade-off is quantified and the conclusion is conditional, naming the measured value at which the alternative would win; the classification explains how the risk tier depends on the deployer's use of the system; the reasoning connects the layers, for example how label delay in the data layer shapes what the technology layer can monitor.

### Evidence (35 %)

- *Insufficient (below 40 %)*: few claims rest on the student's own results; numbers are missing, inconsistent or cannot be traced to a commit.
- *Threshold (40 to 54 %)*: the headline metric and the training time are recorded; at least three of the card's quirks are checked, with counts; the entry includes at least one table or figure from the student's own results.
- *Good (55 to 69 %)*: the packaged code reproduces the notebook's metric from a fresh copy; the quirk table gives, for each quirk, what the card says, what was found, the consequence for the model, and the test or check that now guards it; the service table reports the image size and the p95 latency or throughput, with a dashboard panel; every number traces to a commit.
- *Excellent (70 % and above)*: the numbers are consistent across the written entry, the slides and the video; training-serving consistency is demonstrated, not asserted; the load behind the latency or throughput figures is realistic and justified.

### Insight (30 %)

- *Insufficient (below 40 %)*: the entry describes what was done, or walks through the code, without reflecting on it.
- *Threshold (40 to 54 %)*: at least three of the reflection prompts are addressed, but mostly descriptively.
- *Good (55 to 69 %)*: the entry explains, with specific examples, what surprised the student or went wrong and what they learnt (for example hidden state, an unpinned dependency or a training-serving skew), and what the dashboard cannot show about the model.
- *Excellent (70 % and above)*: the entry states what evidence would change the student's mind, connects the findings to production risks such as silent model failure, and draws consequences for the next steps of the project.

## Limits

- Words beyond 600 are not marked. Tables, figures, code and references do not count towards the 400 to 600 words.
- Slides beyond the eighth content slide are not marked.
- Marking of the video stops at 5:00.
